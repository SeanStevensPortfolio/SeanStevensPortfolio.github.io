My Story:

Why I wanted to transition from studying history to become a User Research and Market Research analyst.

After graduating from the University of Toronto with a degree in Medieval History and Classics I was standing on a bit of a threshold, conflicted on what I wanted to do next. I loved history and it provided me with an engaging vehicle to satisfy my interest and passion in learning about people, ideas and culture. I am a person with many curiosities and a lot of passion for learning new things. At that time I started to feel like my cup was a little full of academia and I was beginning to think about different avenues I could potentially pursue that would satisfy my passion and curiosity in recognizing patterns in human behavior.

After University I worked a lot of different odd jobs and bounced around from place to place trying to find something that fit, and make ends meet of course. It was around this time that a friend of mine gave me the opportunity to learn about working as a web analyst. My family ran a small UX design company contracting for large American corporations out of California. I was able to get the opportunity to work for my family company doing Web analytics at first, and I soon learned about the intersection of UX design and UX research. I found the process fascinating, and it reignited the spark in me to renew my search for a career that would allow me to use my research skills and curiosity in a way that was present and prosperous.

Eventually the company began to close down its operations and I was soon to be out in the wild once again. While I gained a lot of valuable experience and learned a lot during that time, I felt I had only just glimpsed the tip of the ice burg and my curiosity about user research and market research had grown enormously. I realized that there was a lot I still needed to learn, I felt strong in my ability to conduct qualitative focused research and in my ability to translate insights into insightful stories. I felt confident in my communication abilities and design sensibilities, but I was not so confident in the quantitative aspect of working as a user or market researcher. So, I decided to do something about that, and after exploring different options I made the decision to go back to school once again! I enrolled in a Research Analyst post-graduate programme at Humber College.

The programme taught me a lot and really built up my skills and confidence in quantitative research methods. It also helped me refine and hone my skills as a qualitative researcher. I learned how to use data and statistics, with powerful tools like PowerBI, SPSS, and Excel. I learned to how structure, conduct, and implement research surveys and focus groups. I worked on a summative research project conducting policy analysis focused on online learning policy during the COVID-19 pandemic, and in my market research internship with LetsStopAIDS I was proud to build a PowerBI dashboard for the organization that allowed them to compare and identify key insights for the stakeholders and decision makers at LSA.
